# Paper — Training Language Models to Win Debates with Self-Play Improves Judge Accuracy (2024)

## Metadata

arXiv:2409.16636 (v1, Sep 2024).
Samuel Arnesen, David Rein, and Julian Michael (NYU Center for Data Science).

## Impact

Debate's promise is about training: if you reward models for winning debates judged by a weaker overseer, the incentive should push them toward the truth.
Earlier positive results, like [[Paper — Debating with More Persuasive LLMs Leads to More Truthful Answers (2024)]], made debaters stronger only at inference time, and the one prior attempt to actually train debaters found no significant gain in judge accuracy.
This paper trains debaters and finds that as they get better at winning, the judge gets more accurate - and that models trained to persuade without an opponent get better at winning without helping the judge at all.
Its authors present it as the first evidence that debate's core incentive survives training, the setting where it would actually be used.

## Problem it solved

Validating debate as an oversight method requires showing that optimizing debaters to win produces more truthful outcomes, not just more persuasive ones.
Inference-time results left open whether training would find tricks that fool the judge, and the only prior training attempt (Radhakrishnan 2023) found no significant improvement in evaluator accuracy.

## Core idea

Pit copies of a model against each other on questions where one argues the right answer and one the wrong one, reward each for convincing the judge, and check whether the judge ends up right more often as the debaters improve.
The comparison is **consultancy**: a single model argues an assigned side with no opponent, a stand-in for RLHF-style oversight where the evaluator sees only one perspective.
If optimization pressure makes consultants better at winning but not judges more accurate, persuasion is decoupling from truth; if debate keeps them coupled, adversarial structure is doing real work.

## How it's built

Questions come from QuALITY-HARD reading comprehension; following Michael et al. (2023), debaters can read the short story but the judge sees only quotes they choose, with one debater defending the correct answer and the other the best distractor.
Debates have two turns of simultaneous speeches.
The judge is GPT-4-Turbo fine-tuned on human judgments from earlier debate studies, to make it calibrated and less sycophantic - untrained, it agreed with a lone consultant 72% of the time.
Debaters are a long-context Llama-3-8B-Instruct, fine-tuned on human and GPT-4 debate transcripts, then trained with a modified Direct Preference Optimization that uses the judge's confidence as a soft reward, on preference pairs generated by branching self-play.
Consultancy has three variants: single, ensembled (averaging consultancies for both answers), and double (both consultants' speeches shown together, but without either seeing the other's).

## Results

- Training works on its own terms: the fully trained debater wins 67% of rounds against other checkpoints (vs 31% for the supervised-only model) and 82.5% against its own starting point.
- Judge accuracy rises 4 points from the supervised-only debater to the fully trained one (p < 10⁻⁶), even though the judge never sees these debaters' transcripts in training and the debaters are never rewarded for judge accuracy.
- For consultancy, there is no relationship between consultant skill and judge accuracy.
- Accuracy across all checkpoints: debate 77%, double consultancy 75%, ensembled 72%, single 68% - so most of debate's advantage comes from the judge seeing both sides, not from debaters refuting each other.
- Trained debaters use 96% more quoted words than at the start; trained consultants quote less and repeat themselves (98% of second-speech quotes repeated from the first).
- Debater win rates under the trained judge correlate 0.98 with those under an untrained GPT-4o judge, versus 0.51 for consultants - suggesting debaters learn generally convincing arguments, consultants judge-specific tricks.

## Why it endures

It moved debate's evidence from "stronger debaters at test time help" to "training debaters helps," the version that matters for using debate as a training signal.
Its consultancy controls pin down where debate's benefit comes from in this setting - showing both sides and discouraging cheap persuasion during training - while finding that refutation, the mechanism [[Paper — AI Safety via Debate (2018)]] emphasized, barely matters yet.
The contrast between debate and consultancy is also a small-scale picture of how one-sided optimization against an evaluator, as in RLHF, can drift toward persuasion rather than truth.

## Limitations

The authors' own:
- Debate's record in the literature is mixed, so results should be interpreted with caution.
- Stronger models might find strategies that confuse the judge, such as obfuscated arguments.
- The expertise gap comes from access to text, which may be a poor proxy for gaps in reasoning ability.
- Only reading comprehension is tested, and other work finds debate helps less on reasoning tasks.

The gain is 4 points from an 8B model, and debaters are trained against, and accuracy measured with, a single fine-tuned GPT-4-Turbo judge (GPT-4o is used only for the transfer check).
